Who Has To Trade Tomorrow

V. Dimopoulos
BlueShip Research
Working Paper No. 22 · 3 August 2026 · 184 tested, 2 survived, as of 3 August 2026

In one line: 184 trading ideas through the same gates, two alive. The 182 that died include three entire published factor libraries. The survivors share one thing the casualties do not. Somebody is forced to trade against them on a known schedule.

1. The count

Three hundred and sixteen ideas filed. One hundred and eighty-four actually tested. Two survived.

One hundred and twenty-eight tickets errored before producing a verdict, almost all blocked on data I could not obtain for free, and four are still queued. A ticket that never ran earns no place in the denominator. The honest pass rate is two of 184, or 1.1%.

2. Three libraries, no survivors

Microsoft publishes a library of 158 technical alphas. I ran them as one family, so the significance bar rises with the number of attempts. Twenty-nine clear a naive t of 2.5. One clears the deflated bar, a signal counting the days since the high of the last 20 days, at t = 4.46, which also survives the cost stress at 3.68. It is then stopped by a 55% collapse in Sharpe from in-sample to out-of-sample against a 30% limit. Zero are promoted, and the gate that ends it is decay rather than multiplicity. Decomposed, the library is reversal at short horizons and a volatility risk premium wearing 158 costumes. Doubling the costs takes the whole gross edge.

3. The two that lived

A dealer gamma classifier. Dealers who are long gamma hedge against the move and compress the day's range. Dealers who are short gamma chase it and expand the range. The signal predicts the next day's range, not its direction.

A bubble screen. Names that double inside two years crash more often than a control drawn from the same month, at a rate that clears the bar but only just.

Neither is a price factor and neither was clever. Both are decades old and publicly documented.

4. The question that separates them

The distinction is not positioning against price. I believed that and this month proved it too loose.

Commitments of Traders is the canonical positioning dataset. I tested it three ways against a bar of 2.87. The best look reached a t of 1.24, with the sign the wrong way. Dead on arrival.

Somebody is forced. An options dealer who is short gamma does not decide whether to hedge. The hedge is a mechanical consequence of the book he already holds, on a schedule the market can see. A speculator long S&P futures has an opinion, and an opinion poll with a lag is not a mechanism.

The bubble screen is the weaker version. A crowd that doubled its money is not forced to sell, but the unwind is not optional.

Hayek got there first. A signal derived from price is trying to extract information the price already ate.

The operative question is not what the crowd thinks. It is who has to trade tomorrow whether they want to or not.

5. What this predicts

Mechanisms with a compelled counterparty and a visible schedule should keep clearing the bar at a higher rate than anything derived from price alone: dealer hedging, index reconstitution, selling driven by taxes, liquidation driven by redemptions, margin. Sentiment and survey data should keep failing, because holding an opinion imposes no deadline.

A survey or sentiment signal that clears a properly deflated bar under a cost stress would falsify this. It is cheap to test.

6. What is wrong with this paper

The universe is survivorship biased. It is today's large caps, so raw statistics are upper bounds rather than estimates.

Two survivors is a sample of two. Section 4 is a pattern read off two successes and 182 failures, which is the kind of thing this engine exists to be suspicious of. It is a selection rule for what to test next, not an established finding.

The lanes were not chosen at random. I tested BRAIN and Alpha158 because they were available, and a different sample could give a different rate.

The survivors have not been re-tested on data that did not exist when they passed. That test matters most and it is scheduled, not done.

Method. All counts read from the engine's hypothesis store on 3 August 2026 via the read-only query layer. Judged means a ticket reached a verdict of passed, rejected or decayed; errored and queued tickets are excluded from every rate. The gate stack in the swarm lane is a Newey-West t against a family bar deflated by Bonferroni, a ten thousand draw block bootstrap, an in-sample to out-of-sample degradation limit, and a rerun for margin of safety with the gross halved and the costs doubled. Studies in the research lane run a subset and say so in their own papers: the studies of 13F crowding and disclosure text were judged on a deflated Newey-West t with no bootstrap, no out-of-sample split and no cost model. Claiming the full stack applied to all 184 would be false. Every statistic is computed by deterministic code and none by a language model.
