Working Paper No. 30 · 13 August 2026 · closing the family that built the industry

What Remains of Pairs Trading

The trade that seeded modern quant was born at my old employer four decades ago. The museum ran it five ways under a bar that rose with every look, and the verdict is precise: convergence still happens, it earns about 0.3% a year gross on large caps, and the toll is 0.7%. Plus the one test the literature never ran, and what it killed.

V. Dimopoulos  ·  BlueShip Research

Abstract. Pairs trading was born on a Morgan Stanley proprietary desk in the mid-1980s, leaked into academia, and became one of the most tested strategies in finance. This paper closes the museum’s own five-look examination of the family on daily large-cap bars: three cross-sectional conversions through the standard gates, then a faithful pair-level replication after the museum’s own checker caught the first run trading backwards. The verdict is precise. The convergence information still exists, and it is roughly 0.3% a year gross against 0.7% in costs on this universe. Cost-death, not negative edge. The one new result is the delay test the literature had not run: restricting to slow-converging spreads, the natural rescue from execution lag, earns approximately zero even before costs. What genuinely remains of the family is not on the price side at all. It is the book’s third act, risk arbitrage, and it waits on a data vein, not a better selection rule.

1. The shop that started it

In the mid-1980s Morgan Stanley ran a small quantitative group under Nunzio Tartaglia, trading a strategy the rest of the firm barely understood: find two stocks that move together, wait for them to drift apart, bet on the snap back. The group’s alumni seeded modern quantitative finance, David Shaw among them, and the strategy escaped into academia, where Gatev, Goetzmann and Rouwenhorst published the definitive test in 2006, crediting the desk’s practice as the strategy’s origin. I spent years managing risk at the same firm, decades later and several floors away, which makes this family the museum’s most personal exhibit: the trade that built the industry I am applying to, tested with the discipline that industry now demands.

2. Four decades of decay, in the literature’s own numbers

The published record is a staircase going down. Gatev, Goetzmann and Rouwenhorst measured 1.44% per month on the top twenty pairs fully invested, falling to 0.895% when execution waits one day, a drop that already implies roughly 162 basis points of round-trip friction. Do and Faff extended the sample and watched the staircase: 0.86% per month through 1988, 0.37% through 2002, 0.24% after, and negative after realistic costs, with the after-cost survivor confined to well-matched within-industry pairs at around 30 basis points a month. Engelberg, Gao and Jagannathan showed the profits decay exponentially in event time and concentrate in illiquid names. A 2024 study found liquidity screens remove roughly 60% of gross profit, and separate work finds the pairwise cointegration the textbook version assumes is mostly not there. Every number above is the literature’s own; the museum’s contribution starts where the staircase ends.

3. Five looks, one bar that kept rising

The museum ran the family five ways, all declared as one family so the significance bar climbed with every look, from a t-statistic of 2.50 required at the first to 3.03 at the fifth. Three cross-sectional conversions went through the standard gates first: classic distance divergence, a slow-spread variant, and a market-residual variant. All three died, but the autopsy was informative. The divergence signal carries real ordering information, an information coefficient with a t-statistic of 2.52 and perfectly monotone quintiles, with an implied information ratio ceiling of 0.22, and in every look most of what little profit existed came from the least liquid third of a large-cap universe, 94 to 96% in the two distance-based ones.

Then the museum’s checker earned its seat twice. First it ruled the cross-sectional design an unfair test of pair-level trading, so a faithful replication was built: mutual minimum-distance pairs, two-sigma entries, convergence exits, one-day execution lag, 247 overlapping books, 8,051 round trips, average holding 54 days. Second, it caught the replication’s entry direction inverted before publication, and the verdicts were re-adjudicated on the corrected run, which flipped the first run’s verdict from an alarming loss to the truth. The corrected numbers: the headline book nets minus 0.37% a year, and the slow-spread book minus 0.57%, both nowhere near a bar of 3.03 and both negative even in sample.

4. Cost-death, not negative edge

The framing matters and the museum’s checker enforced it. Running the book in both directions brackets the truth: convergence still earns roughly 0.30% a year gross on this universe, against 0.67% a year in conservative costs. The strategy is not wrong about the market. Diverged twins still converge. The rent is simply higher than the harvest, by about a factor of two, on the only universe where the capacity to matter exists. On smaller and less liquid names the literature still finds gross profit, and the museum’s own liquidity attribution agrees, which is the same finding wearing different clothes: convergence pays where trading is hardest, meaning it was always compensation for providing liquidity, not free information.

5. The result the literature had not run

The one genuinely new number is the delay test. The obvious rescue from execution lag is selection: if a one-day delay destroys fast-converging spreads, restrict to spreads with mean-reversion half-lives of ten to sixty days, which a delayed trader can still catch. The literature documents the damage of delay but had not tested the rescue. The museum did, twice, once cross-sectionally and once at pair level with fixed formation anchors after the checker found the first design absorbed exactly the slow divergences it meant to select. The answer: the slow-spread book’s gross return is approximately zero. Slowness does not protect the edge, because the slow spreads carry no edge to protect. Latency selection fails before costs even enter, which closes the family’s last honest escape route on daily bars.

6. What remains

Three things, honestly inventoried. The literature’s within-industry survivor, around 30 basis points a month after costs on well-matched pairs, is their claim, small, and untested here. The illiquid pocket is real in both their data and this engine’s attribution, and it cannot absorb size, which is why it survives. And the largest remainder is not a price strategy at all: the pairs textbook’s third act applies the same spread machinery to merger arbitrage, where the flow is forced and the museum’s own constraint audit already queued the enabling data vein first in line. The family’s sixth look, whenever it comes, must bring a new mechanism. The museum’s checker put it plainly: more selection rules are not the binding constraint.

7. What this does not establish

The universe is survivorship-biased current large caps, which flatters convergence specifically, since a divergence that ended in a delisting never enters the panel; every gross figure above is therefore an upper bound, and the rejections are robust in the only direction that matters. The verdicts are one engine, one universe, daily bars, and say nothing about intraday or execution-privileged versions of the trade, which live beyond this museum’s stated boundary. The literature’s surviving pockets are reported as their claims, not replicated here. And the family’s dead ends are recorded in the store with their reasons, so nobody here retests them by accident, which is the entire point of keeping a museum.

Method
Five store verdicts in one declared family, adjudicated 11 August 2026 at family-deflated bars from 2.50 to 3.03. Pair-level lab: 252-day formation, 126-day trading, two-sigma entry, zero-cross exit, one-day execution lag, 5 basis points per traded dollar plus borrow, committed-capital convention; 247 overlapping books, 8,051 round trips. The checker’s two interventions (design ruling, inverted entry line caught before publication) are documented in the cycle report.
