Wrong, According to Whom?

Blind checkers judged 3 of 113 findings by this desk’s AI review agents flatly wrong. On the same 26 sweep findings, an earlier AI verifier that asked a different question had called 7 unsupported, so an error rate depends on who checks and what they ask.

V. Dimopoulos  ·  BlueShip Research

Abstract. A registered blind re-check of 113 August findings by this desk’s AI review agents judged 3 flatly wrong. On the same 26 sweep findings, an earlier AI verifier, asking whether each was shown rather than true, called 7 unsupported; the blind checkers called none flatly wrong. An agent’s error rate sets how much checking its output needs, and depends on who checks and what they ask.

1. The missing number

A LinkedIn list gathered seven Anthropic and OpenAI stories of AI agents in investment research. Four report speed and four a correctness figure from a model test. None says how often output was wrong in live use or sent back by a reviewer. I measured that number on my own agents.

2. The re-check

Two August rounds supplied the findings, one reviewer per published note and a sweep by a few agents over many notes. Two blind checkers judged each finding, and a third settled their 10 splits.

The questions differ, yet the layers are not noise. The checkers rated the fix unsound on 4 of the verifier’s 7 unsupported findings, and the skeptics rejected 16 of the 17 the checkers judged not fully right. My record read the verifier’s first sample, 5 unsupported of 8, as a sweep about 63% false positive; the checkers judged those 5 findings 3 right and 2 partly right. Which layer is right needs a human-graded sample I have not drawn.

I shipped the one wrong per-note correction with 30 others and left one right correction unshipped, one miss each way and too few to grade my shipping step. A false claim has stood on a live page since 21 August.

5. What to ask for

Beside an agent’s speed, ask how many outputs a person checked, how many were wrong and how many a reviewer sent back. Then ask who checked, against what question, and who checked the checker.

6. What this does not claim

The checkers are Claude agents, like the reviewers, so a blind spot they share is invisible; blind is not the same as right. The two rounds differ in setup and kind of finding and are not independent, so their gap tests no design. I audited no vendor; a firm that publishes no check number may keep one. Two rounds of one desk’s agents set no error rate for anyone else.

Method
Registered protocol docs/research/agent_check_rate/00_protocol.md, sha256 6725c84790474e13b8be92ffd210ec2fedac2255f493ee7f3ebb62eb094d2273, fixed before any sample was drawn; addenda 00_protocol_addenda.md, sha256 06d8f560a391d50f8e4abc0d30349a763e39b143abdf24f95c741c3a97fda637. The results file records the addenda hash before Addendum 2 (56766f2436cae1c11749c2fca4401dc33c8e4aa82f3dfa563ac407e2a00f272e). Rounds: the per-note library review of 21 August 2026 (39 notes, one reviewer each against lens files routed by subject; 145 findings, of which all 32 graded correction were checked and 30 drawn from the other 113 (145 less 32) by a seeded hash rule stratified by severity) and the library-gate sweep of 19 August 2026 (8 lens-reading agents, 101 findings; all 51 its authors weighted load-bearing were checked). Checkers were not shown how a finding had been graded or any later version of the page; each finding was judged against the public page at the last commit before any fix could land: holds, partly (something is wrong there, but not as stated), does not hold, or cannot tell (none). Suggestions were judged on their factual premise. Not fully right is partly plus does not hold. Intervals are Wilson 95% and percentages round half up. Kappa is unweighted Cohen’s kappa before adjudication, over four categories between the two checkers, three in the verifier comparison and two in the skeptic comparison. Fisher tests are exact and two-sided; they describe gaps and gate nothing. On not fully right, per-note corrections against all sweep findings give p 0.0016, and against the sweep’s 29 corrections, 8 of them not fully right, p 0.037. The 51 sweep findings come from 8 agents on 18 notes, so they are not independent draws, and the rounds are not independent of each other: 8 of the 32 corrections come from notes whose reviewers were handed sweep findings. The all-113 row adds a census of corrections, a stratified draw and a census of the sweep’s load-bearing findings, so it gives counts and estimates no rate. The verifier comparison maps confirmed, partially confirmed and unsupported to holds, partly and does not hold over 26 text-matched links kept after Addendum 1 dropped one link whose result quotes an earlier sweep; the first-sample row is the registered sample of 8, before that drop, so it is not a subset of the 26; over all 27 links before the drop, agreement is 8 of 27 (kappa 0.01). The skeptic comparison counts survives with holds or partly and rejected with does not hold; read instead as rejected against not fully right, agreement is 24 of 36 (kappa 0.35), and rejection goes with not fully right (Fisher p 0.02). The skeptic figures, the all-113 counts, and the layer shares, intervals and same-finding counts in the second table are descriptive and computed for this note. Shipping is read from a record sealed before the re-check: 28 corrections through the 21 August queue and 3 mechanical fixes; shipped means in that queue or among its mechanical fixes. Honesty
This desk’s engine runs on Anthropic’s Claude models, and I use OpenAI’s ChatGPT as a red team. The reviewers measured here, and the checkers and adjudicators who measured them, are Claude agents, and Claude agents ran the study and drafted this note at my direction. Three of the seven stories are Anthropic’s, two of the OpenAI pages benchmark OpenAI’s models against Claude models, and one customer’s own release says its portfolios invest in Anthropic; nothing here ranks either vendor, and every vendor figure is vendor-reported. Limits recorded in the addenda and results: on 10 findings (2 per-note, 8 sweep) a checker saw a later fix or result; the session harness put my memory index in front of checkers against the protocol, and checkers on 10 sweep findings saw its line calling the sweep about 63% false positive, which pushes toward wrong while the verdicts went the other way; the sweep round was checked against today’s code, since the private repository has no commits for that window; the sealed shipping record was opened early, by the verifier of the extraction and before any checker ran, and no checker or adjudicator read it; the verifier comparison rests on a text-similarity mapping, with 3 low-margin links. Without the 8 sweep findings where a checker saw a later fix or result, the sweep is 2.3% flatly wrong and 41.9% not fully right, of 43.
