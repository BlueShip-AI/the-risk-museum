Working Paper No. 46 · BlueShip Research · 12 October 2026 · a memo to the pods

The Midterm Desk

What the 3 November midterm can and cannot move, pod by pod.

V. Dimopoulos  ·  BlueShip Research

Abstract. A memo to this desk’s paper-floor pod managers on what the 3 November midterm can move.

1. The event

A midterm changes Congress, not the executive, so its channels are legislation, appropriations, oversight and confirmations, and its outcome is a composition, unified or divided. The president’s party lost a chamber in 10 of the last 25 midterms (lab1), and the lame duck acts before any new majority sits. In the week after the vote, the median absolute market move ranks at the 77th percentile of ordinary weeks for presidential elections (p 0.002) and the 67th for midterms (p 0.077).

2. Sector pods

Across 25 midterms the industry cross-section sits inside its null band in every window. Any exposure would sit in names inside the sector hedge, which the desk’s unscored 10-K risk-factor measure, due to freeze on 23 October, is built to find. The one midterm that hurt the paper desks, 2022, had CPI on day +1 and one momentum signal shared by the seven sector desks, so nothing ties that week to the vote (lab2).

3. Macro and rates

The curve is the one midterm rates cell below p 0.01. Over twelve midterms the 2s10s slope flattened by a mean of 9.8 bp in the week after the vote, in eleven of the twelve (lab3). Scaled by volatility, only 1994, 1998 and 2002 pass two standard deviations (lab3_refute), an observation, not a mechanism, and eight of 90 event-cell p-values clear 0.05 where 4.5 would by chance. Congress holds funding, the debt limit and confirmations (Table 1).

4. Systematic equity

Two of the 24 midterm summary cells fall below 0.05, both of them factor cells, against 1.2 expected by chance (critic_wp46). Section 2’s unscored measure is built to find the names that carry event variance inside a market-neutral hedge.

5. Event-driven and credit

The Baa-over-Treasuries spread shows no detectable excess move around twenty elections, but the series is smoothed, so its silence says little about CDS (lab3). Merger review sits with the antitrust agencies, which a midterm does not change; what it changes is the Senate that confirms their heads.

6. Central risk

Day 0 is 4 November, with no FOMC meeting that week. The floor has no event-day rule and relies on its standing limits (Method). Median cross-desk dispersion in midterm reaction weeks was 0.809 against 0.672 in ordinary weeks, with three of five events above it, and 1.534 in presidential weeks (lab2).

What this does not claim

Nothing here takes a direction by party or by outcome, and the sample cannot price a change of control. The desk mappings in Table 1 are judgement, and its calendar facts come from the desk files of 29 September. The floor panel is survivorship-biased while the French panels are not, and all returns are gross of costs. The 174 p-values are uncorrected and exploratory. Nothing here is advice.

Method
Election day is the Tuesday after the first Monday in November; day 0 is the first trading day strictly after it, which handles the years when the exchange closed on election day, through 1966 for midterms and through 1980 for presidential elections. Windows are pre [-20,-1], reaction [0,+4] and post [+5,+24] trading days. Lab 1 compounds value-weighted Ken French industry and factor returns over each window, takes the cross-sectional standard deviation across industries or the absolute factor move, and places the midterm median against one fixed tiling of non-overlapping blocks of the same length from July 1926, with a two-sided permutation p from 10,000 draws at seed 20261007; the composition split reshuffles the chamber-lost label across the 25 midterms. Lab 2 regenerates the ten paper desks of pipeline/trading_floor.py in memory, three systematic and seven within-sector 12-1 momentum quartile long/short, cumulates each desk’s unlevered return over the windows and standardises against every same-length window in its 2005 to 2026 history. Lab 3 differences FRED levels across the same windows and tests the event mean against non-overlapping windows by permutation; the slope is DGS10 minus DGS2 on common dates. Table 1 is the policy calendar’s desk mapping and asserts no sign. The floor’s standing limits are risk-parity weights with a 0.5 risk budget for each of the systematic and sector groups, a 10% annualized volatility target, a 2.0x gross cap, and a ladder that halves exposure 5% below the year’s high and flattens at 7.5%.

Honesty
Every sample is the whole population since the data begin, and it is small: 25 midterms for lab 1, 5 for lab 2, and 9 to 32 elections per cell for lab 3. Labs 1 to 3 were not pre-registered, so every cell in Table 2 is exploratory. Labs 1 and 3 compute no power for their placement cells, so a cell inside its null band is an absence of evidence, not a null; only the day-after row carries a minimum detectable effect, 73.6 bp. Labs 1 and 3 report 174 uncorrected p-values; 23 fall below 0.05 against 8.7 expected by chance; the Bonferroni bar is 0.00029, and the one cell that clears it is the unconditional VIX-against-absolute-10-year-change correlation, not an election cell. The two lowest election p-values, 0.002 each, are presidential cells, the market reaction and RMW before the vote; section 1 cites the first only as a contrast. The critic’s first ledger counted 18 below 0.05 by omitting the five correlation p-values below 0.05 from its count; the script was corrected on 12 October and the figures here are its output. DGS10, DGS2 and the slope share legs. No scheduled release was purged from the windows: FOMC statements fell on day 0 in 2010 and on day +1 in 2018, CPI on day +1 in 2022, and the post window overlaps the December FOMC in most years. No subperiod split was run. The paper floor has no macro or credit desk, so sections 3 and 5 rest on labs 1 and 3, not on the floor. No prediction-venue figure appears anywhere in this note. Lab 3’s unconditional pool includes the election windows, which biases its tests toward the null; lab 1’s ex-event check moves no placement by more than one percentile point. Lab 2’s 2022 reaction window overlaps the October CPI release of 10 November; Healthcare’s loss is the second-lowest five-day window in that desk’s history, percentile 0.04; the book itself was flat through 2022 because the annual stop had taken exposure to zero on 23 May, so its 0.00% there is an overlay artefact. The composition record behind the split is hand-coded and was checked on 12 October 2026 against the House and Senate party-division records; every label matched, with 1930 coded on the chamber that organized and 2002 on the June 2001 switch. Three refuter corrections are applied: the split thresholds run from 0.21% to 2.82%, lab 2’s window counts are 5,202 to 5,469, and Healthcare’s week is the second-lowest, not the lowest. The Item 1A measure is status, not result: the lexicon, due 9 October, is not yet approved, the 300-sentence labels are due 16 October, and the freeze is planned for 23 October; its power at the family bar is 0.831 with five events and only if exposed names move about 1.5 times as much. No code under swarm/, pipeline/ or deploy/ was changed; no threshold moved.
