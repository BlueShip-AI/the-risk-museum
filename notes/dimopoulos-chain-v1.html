The Dimopoulos Chain: Options-Dealer Gamma States and the
Incremental Forecasting of Realized Volatility Beyond Asymmetric GARCH

V. Dimopoulos
BlueShip Research
Working Paper No. 1 · 12 July 2026

In one line: I built a model to predict market swings and named it after myself. A 1993 model already beat it.

Abstract. I construct the Dimopoulos Chain, a nine-state Markov model over the interaction of options-dealer gamma terciles and realized-volatility terciles, and test whether its state-conditional distributions carry information for one-day-ahead volatility beyond established econometric benchmarks. Out of sample (2019–2026, 1,794 days, refit monthly with no look-ahead), the chain’s states add significant information beyond the VIX (Newey–West t = 4.34) and beyond GARCH(1,1) (t = 3.19); their increment over the HAR benchmark reaches only t = 2.03, short of the promotion gate, and vanishes once the asymmetric GJR-GARCH model of Glosten, Jagannathan and Runkle (1993) enters the baseline (t = 1.02). I read the leverage effect GJR captures as a reduced form of the dealer-hedging mechanism the chain measures directly, and record a verdict of PARTIAL. The name is not earned at the strictest bar. Two results survive independently of the ranking. Every econometric forecast in the stack beats the market’s implied forecast by a wide margin, and the state map isolates one of nine states in which the implied-minus-realized variance premium turns negative.

Keywords: volatility forecasting, options-dealer gamma, Markov regime states, GJR-GARCH, variance risk premium, Newey–West inference.

1. Introduction

Black-Scholes prices every option with a single constant volatility; markets do not behave that way. Volatility arrives in regimes, and one structural driver of those regimes is options-dealer positioning. When dealers are long gamma their hedging pins the market; when they are short gamma the same hedging amplifies every move. The BlueShip pipeline had already validated this positioning signal, which predicts next-day trading range beyond the VIX at t = 7.4. This paper asks whether the signal, promoted to a forecasting model, adds information beyond the standard econometric volatility benchmarks, and therefore whether it earns a name.

2. Model and data

The Dimopoulos Chain is a Markov chain over nine market states, formed as the interaction of a dealer-gamma tercile and a realized-volatility tercile; its state-conditional distributions forecast next-day volatility. The house rule is that a name is earned by surviving the gates, as a Sharpe ratio would be; a model that does not survive is retained as an exhibit under its own name. The evaluation is out of sample, 2019–2026, 1,794 days, with all forecasts refit monthly and no look-ahead. The promotion gate is a Newey–West t-statistic of 2.5 on the information a model adds beyond a stated baseline.

3.2 The GJR-GARCH baseline

The asymmetric GJR-GARCH model of Glosten, Jagannathan and Runkle (1993) is the single best forecaster in the stack, and once it enters the baseline the chain’s states add nothing significant. The likely reason is that the leverage effect GJR captures, in which down moves raise volatility more than up moves, is a reduced form of the dealer mechanism itself. A fall in the market induces put buying, which pushes dealers short gamma, which amplifies volatility. A 33-year-old econometric model was already pricing the shadow of the structure the chain measures directly.

4. Findings that survive

4.1 The variance risk premium

Every competent forecast in the stack, GJR, HAR, the chain and their combinations, beats the implied forecast by a wide and significant margin. Options are traded against implied volatility, which no econometric model sets. The gap between the two is the variance risk premium, and the timing of that gap is the live research program.

4.2 The state map

The state map locates the danger precisely. Measured by state, implied-minus-realized variance is positive in eight of nine states, with hit rates up to 90% where dealers are pinned, and negative in exactly one, where dealers are exposed and volatility is already high. That single state of nine is where short-volatility strategies fail, and it is identifiable a day in advance.

Postscript, and the point of the exhibit. Three further versions followed, and the pipeline retired every one. Version 1.1 cleared a HAR + VIX + plain-GARCH baseline (t = 2.83) but not the stricter GJR-augmented one (t = 1.02). Version 1.5 replaced the linear emissions with a neural network; it forecast worse, not better (t = −4.37). Version 3 conditioned shrunk state corrections on the term structure and event arrivals over a log HAR + VIX + GJR backbone (separate paper), and failed as well. Four versions, one name, and the same gate refused each (a gate the author wrote). That the model carried my name bought it no leniency. The record of its four burials is the exhibit; the model never was.
