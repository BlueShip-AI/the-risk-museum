Reading a Book: a risk teardown

by V. Dimopoulos · Research note #11 · July 25, 2026

A portfolio is a set of claims. The manager claims the returns come from his ideas; the risk seat's job is to find out what the book actually owns. This note performs that job start to finish on a single book — the same sequence a platform risk desk runs when a new PM's positions land on the screen: take the book, decompose its risk, hand back findings. This book is hypothetical and illustrative — a teaching example built to be taken apart, not a real position, a recommendation, or advice.

2 — Factor decomposition

The constant-weight book's daily return is regressed on the Fama-French five factors plus Carhart momentum (Newey-West standard errors), over 5,384 days from 2005-01-04 to 2026-05-29. The intercept is the residual alpha; the slopes are what the book owns whether it meant to or not.

ExposureBook beta
Market (Mkt-RF)+0.56
Size (SMB)-0.03
Value (HML)-0.17
Profitability (RMW)-0.13
Investment (CMA)-0.50
Momentum (Mom)+0.03

R² is 61.3%: 61.3% of the book's daily variance is these six factors, leaving only 38.7% idiosyncratic. Annualized residual alpha is +8.1% with a t-statistic of 3.75 — positive and in-sample significant, but see the survivorship caveat above: it is a ceiling, not a track record. The exposures, intended versus uninvited:

  • Market (Mkt-RF) beta +0.56. The book's net dollar exposure is only +30%, yet its realized market beta is 0.56 — the shorts are low-beta defensives, so netting dollars did not net out direction. In beta terms the book is about 1.9× more market-directional than its net book implies. Unintended.
  • Investment (CMA) beta -0.50: a tilt toward aggressive, high-reinvestment growth names (long mega-cap tech) funded by short conservative payers. A style bet, not stock selection. Unintended.
  • Value (HML) beta -0.17: net short value / long growth. A style bet, not stock selection. Unintended.
  • Profitability (RMW) beta -0.13: mildly short quality — the shorts are the profitable defensives. A style bet, not stock selection. Unintended.
  • Size (SMB) beta -0.03: negligible — both sleeves are large-cap, so the book is correctly size-neutral. Intended (clean).

6 — Findings and recommendations

What a risk desk would tell this PM — the three issues that matter, and a concrete fix for each:

  1. Hidden market beta. The book runs a 0.56 market beta on +30% net dollars — roughly 1.9× more directional than the net book suggests, because every short is a low-beta defensive. Fix: size to a beta target, not a dollar target — swap some defensive shorts for higher-beta names or overlay an index short until realized beta matches the intended net.
  2. Risk hides in one name. NVDA is 16.0% of gross dollars but 41.4% of portfolio variance; the book is diversified by dollars and concentrated by risk (effective bets 9.3 by dollars, far fewer by risk). Fix: cap single-name risk contribution, not just single-name weight — trim NVDA or add an offsetting position.
  3. It is a style bet wearing a stock-picker's coat. Only 38.7% of daily variance is idiosyncratic; 61.3% is the six factors, led by an Investment (CMA) beta of -0.50 — a large long-growth / short-conservative tilt. Fix: neutralize the CMA/growth tilt if the thesis is single-name selection, or own it as the actual strategy and budget risk to it deliberately.
Why this hangs in the museum: the teardown is the product. Anyone can quote a book's net exposure; the work is showing that +30% net dollars carry a 0.56 market beta, that 16.0% of gross is 41.4% of the risk, and that a book billed as stock-picking is 61.3% factor by variance. Same discipline the museum's machine runs nightly on its own strategies — here run on a book, on the page.
