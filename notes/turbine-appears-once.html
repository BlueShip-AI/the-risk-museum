The Turbine Appears Once

V. Dimopoulos
BlueShip Research
Working Paper No. 23 · 3 August 2026 · scoring a 2024 forecast on a 2026 record

In one line: I scored every claim in Leopold Aschenbrenner’s Situational Awareness whose window has closed. On forward calls he is 13 right, 3 partial, 7 wrong. He was right about the machines and wrong about what they would cost, and the word “turbine” appears once in 165 pages.

1. What I did, and the rules I set before I started

In June 2024 Leopold Aschenbrenner published Situational Awareness: The Decade Ahead, 165 pages arguing that AGI arrives around 2027 and that the constraint is industrial rather than intellectual. In July 2026 the hedge fund he built on that thesis was liquidated after margin calls. That sequence invites a cheap article. This is not one.

The rules were fixed before I read. Only closed windows are scored. Roughly sixty of his claims are dated 2027, 2028, 2030 or "the end of the decade," including his headline thesis. None of them is due, so none carries a verdict here. Judging a forecast before its resolution date is the exact error my own forecast wing exists to avoid. Retrospective claims are scored as sourcing accuracy, not prediction skill, and labelled that way. Where the evidence is thin I say the evidence is thin rather than forcing a verdict to fill a cell. That leaves 27 claims about the future whose windows have closed.

On the only part that tests prediction, he is 13 right, 3 partial, 7 wrong, 4 undetermined. For a document two years old that makes dated quantitative claims, that is a respectable record.

3. Where he was right, including where he was too cautious

He wrote that Nvidia would do over $200 billion of revenue in calendar 2025 against a consensus on the sell side he named as $120 to $130 billion. Nvidia's fiscal 2026 came in at $215.9 billion. He named the consensus, named his own number, and beat both.

He tabulated a 2026 cluster at roughly a million H100-equivalents and about a gigawatt. In January 2026, Colossus 2 came online at about a gigawatt. That is a forecast of a physical object two years ahead, and the object exists.

On aggregate AI investment he was wrong in the direction nobody expects. He projected roughly $500 billion for 2026. Actual hyperscaler AI infrastructure spend is running near $725 billion. The number most mocked in the document was too small.

4. Where he was wrong

His timing for revenue on the demand side ran about a year late. He put OpenAI at a $10 billion run rate by late 2024 or early 2025; it got there in June 2025. He projected a company in big tech at $100 billion of AI revenue by mid-2026; Microsoft, the only one of the three he named that discloses a comparable figure, reported $37 billion.

A fairness note cuts toward him on the second one. His assumed doubling rate was, if anything, too slow. Anthropic went from a run rate of roughly $1 billion of revenue to $47 billion in seventeen months. He asked the right question of the wrong firms.

5. The turbine appears once

Aschenbrenner identified the binding constraint correctly and early. "Probably the single biggest constraint on the supply-side will be power," he wrote, and much of the industry conversation since traces to him saying it.

Then he priced it. Gas plant capex "under $1000 per kW." Combined cycle plants buildable "in about two years."

Neither survived contact. Gas turbine prices are reported up roughly 300% in three years. Current analyst estimates put GE Vernova's heavy-duty turbine equipment alone near $790 per kW and its aeroderivative units near $1,800.

I searched the full text. The word "turbine" appears once in 165 pages, in a subordinate clause: "The harder part would be building enough generators / turbines; this wouldn't be trivial, but it seems doable." That single clause is where both of his power misses live. He saw the constraint and waved at its supply chain in eleven words.

This is not evidence that he is careless. The essay is about capability trajectories and it is entitled to compress the industrial detail. His claim about the physics held. His claim about the price did not, because the price lives in a supply chain he did not model. When a forecast has a physical bottleneck in it, the forecast is only as good as your model of the bottleneck.

6. Where his claims and mine touch the same object

On 1 August 2026 I submitted fourteen pre-registered forecasts with binding resolution criteria, and the slate froze at submission. Three price things his essay also discusses. The comparison is about form rather than merit.

Power. He wrote that power is the biggest constraint, undated, with no threshold, closing on three modal verbs: it “can, must, and will be solved.” I priced one auction. PJM’s 2029/30 base residual auction clears at or above $325.00 per megawatt-day, 72%, resolving on PJM’s own published results. Same underlying claim. One of the two can be graded by a stranger who does not care about either of us.

Hyperscaler capex. This is the tightest comparison available, the same four companies and the same line item. He gave 2024 dollar thresholds per company and went three for four. His miss was Meta at $39.23 billion against his “$40 billion plus,” a 2% shortfall that becomes 7% if you exclude finance lease principal, and which definition applied was never specified. My version specifies the exclusions in writing, before the fact, because I read his and saw where the ambiguity was. Note the direction of aggression, which does not flatter me. His implied growth rate was 100% a year. Mine is 20%, and I still price it at 62%.

Chips and China. Here the finding is an absence. Across 165 pages, “rare earth” appears zero times, “chokepoint” zero times, and “ASML” zero times. His supply risk runs in one direction, from American capability outward. Two of my fourteen forecasts price the chokepoints running the other way.

7. What this does not show

My slate has resolved zero of fourteen. On 1 August 2026 I submitted fourteen pre-registered forecasts with binding resolution criteria. No accuracy comparison between us is computable, and I am not offering one.

Being resolvable is not the same as being right. A forecast with a binding criterion and a date can be graded, which is a different virtue from being correct. If my fourteen come back at ten wrong, I will have been wrong in public on schedule.

A directional essayist and a pre-registered forecaster are doing different jobs. He was writing to change what people believe about the decade, and by that standard the essay worked. I am not pretending he failed at a task he did not set himself.

And the fund is not evidence about the essay. A liquidation is a statement about leverage, correlation and margin. It says nothing about whether AGI arrives in 2027. The sixty undue claims stay undue.

Method. Every claim was extracted from the June 2024 PDF with page citations and classified as checkable now or not yet due before any scoring. Verification used primary sources where they exist: SEC filings and company results for revenue and capex, PJM's published auction releases for capacity prices, EIA series for electricity. Trade press is flagged inline as secondary wherever it was the only source available. The full extraction and scorecard, including the roughly sixty undue claims left unscored, is available on request.
