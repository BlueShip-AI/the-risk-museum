Nothing Could Have Hedged This Book

V. Dimopoulos
BlueShip Research
Working Paper No. 21 · 3 August 2026

In one line: The two legs of the Situational Awareness book were correlated at 0.70, which caps any hedge between them at about 29% risk reduction no matter how it is sized. The failure was instrument selection, not sizing and not leverage. Every figure comes from their own filing and public prices. The disclosed short book is partial, the one fact that could overturn this.

1. The claim

The consensus reading, put well by Tae Kim on 30 July, is that the positions were right and the leverage was wrong: at four times gross, a drawdown became a liquidation. I agree, and the proximate cause was margin.

One layer underneath, leverage is judged against net exposure, and net exposure is a model. My first draft argued the hedge was sized wrong. The arithmetic showed the opposite.

2. What the filing shows

The Q1 2026 13F, filed 18 May 2026, shows forty-two positions totalling $13.68bn.

The long book is $3.86bn of AI infrastructure in small and mid caps. The eight largest names, about 91% of it, are Bloom Energy, Sandisk, CoreWeave, IREN, Core Scientific, Applied Digital, Riot and CleanSpark.

The disclosed short side is $8.46bn of notional in puts on mega cap semiconductors and Oracle: the VanEck semiconductor ETF, Nvidia, Oracle, Broadcom, AMD, Micron, TSMC, ASML and Intel. There is also $1.36bn of calls, so the disclosed structure is more layered than one basket against another.

Both sides are the AI capital cycle, one with high beta and one with low beta.

3. The hedging ceiling

I formed baskets of daily returns of the two sides from adjusted daily closes, June 2025 through July 2026. The relevant quantity is how much risk the short basket can remove from the long one, at best. No sizing decision beats that ceiling.

Window Correlation Ceiling: most any hedge can remove Dollar neutral achieved
Before July 0.70 28.6% 27.2%
July 0.95 67.3% 42.8%

Before the stress, the most shorting these semiconductors could ever have removed was 28.6% of that long book’s volatility, and the dollar-neutral version achieved 27.2%, or 95% of the ceiling. The pair of baskets is the problem.

Weighting by the filing's own dollar amounts changes nothing material: correlation 0.76 and 27.6% removed before July.

4. Why 0.70 is the worst number to have

0.70 is high enough that both sides fall together when the theme unwinds, and in July the correlation rose to 0.95.

It is also too low for the short to do much work. Half of the long book's variance is not explained by the short book, and that half you keep however the hedge is sized. The book carried about three quarters of the volatility of being long the high beta leg, a property of the instruments chosen and not of the risk management.

5. What that does to four times gross

Four times gross with roughly flat net means about two dollars long against two dollars short. On legs that retain three quarters of their outright risk, that book carries roughly 1.5 times the volatility of being outright long the high beta leg, unlevered.

6. The cheap check

The check is a regression of the long book on the short book, run on public prices before the position is sized. The awkward middle near 0.7 is where a book looks hedged on the exposure report and is not hedged in the returns.

7. What would overturn this

The disclosed short book is partial, and this is the caveat that matters most. A 13F captures long positions and options, not short stock. If the undisclosed portion was large and uncorrelated with the long leg, my correlations overstate the problem. I cannot rule that out.

They held puts, not linear shorts. Treating a dollar of put notional as a dollar of linear short is an approximation that runs against the fund: a put's short exposure rises as the underlying falls, so the real hedge probably worked better in July than my linear proxy shows.

The filing was already stale. A 5.6% position in Nebius carries a May event date and a position near 20% in a micro cap carries a June one. Neither is in the March filing.

I have not verified the fund's size, leverage or returns. Those come from press reporting. Section 5 depends on the reported four times gross.

Tae Kim, "The Big Lesson from the Implosion of Leopold Aschenbrenner's $20 Billion Situational Awareness Hedge Fund," 30 July 2026.

Method. Baskets of daily returns, weighted equally and by filing value, of the disclosed long names and of the names underlying the disclosed puts. Correlations and betas by ordinary least squares on daily returns. Volatility is the standard deviation of daily returns annualised at 252 days. The hedging ceiling is the standard minimum variance result: a hedged book cannot fall below the unhedged volatility times the square root of one minus the squared correlation.
