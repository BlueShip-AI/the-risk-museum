How I work

Decide what would change your mind before you look at the answer. Risk management taught me that habit. It is easy to claim in an interview and hard to prove, which is why this whole site exists as the proof. Thresholds are fixed in code before a test runs, and moving one after seeing a result is treated as what it is.

A number a language model produced is not a number. Models here read papers, write feature code and critique results. Deterministic Python computes every statistic. No model is ever asked to estimate a Sharpe ratio or a t-statistic, because a plausible number is worse than no number.

Multiplicity is charged honestly, including against myself. Every look at the same data raises the bar for the next one. When I found that one of my own tests was underpowered, the fix was to run it again at proper power and pay the deflator, not to retire the weak test and keep its easier bar.

Assume the data layer is wrong until it is checked. The failures that cost me the most here were never statistical. One was a regulatory filing panel truncated per company at a date that depended on filing rate, the very variable under test. Another was a study that drifted because one input still read a nightly-refreshed cache. Both were invisible in every summary statistic.

Survivorship is stated, not implied. The universe is names that lived into today’s index file, so the manias that actually died are the missing cell. Every result here says so, including the ones it weakens.

What this is not

Not a track record. No capital is at risk here, and a backtest cannot supply what a live book does. The survivors are held back rather than shown, because a published edge stops working. What is on display is the process, and everything it rejected.
