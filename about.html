About · who built this and why

I wanted to find out whether the process holds up when nobody is grading it

I am Vasilios Dimopoulos. I spent my career in enterprise and strategic risk at Morgan Stanley, I am moving into quantitative research, and I built this site to test whether I could run a research process to the standard a desk would demand, then publish what it produced whether or not it flattered me.

V. Dimopoulos  ·  BlueShip Research

Why I built it

Risk management taught me one habit above all others: decide what would change your mind before you look at the answer. That habit is easy to describe in an interview and hard to prove, because most quantitative work is shown only when it worked. The published record of this field is a survivorship-biased sample of its own output, which is a strange thing for a discipline that spends so much time warning about survivorship bias.

So I built the engine I would want to have working for me, and I inverted the convention: the failures get published, with their reasons and their numbers. What you see here is the whole sample, not the flattering tail of it. Two ideas out of hundreds have survived the gates. Both are recorded, and so is everything that died.

The gates never move to accommodate a result. That is the only rule that makes the rest of it worth reading, because a bar you can lower on a good day is not a bar.

What is actually here

An engine that generates hypotheses, tests them against a significance bar that rises with every attempt at the same idea, stresses the survivors for costs, and files each verdict with its reason. A shelf of research notes on what it found, including several whose conclusion is that the thing I set out to measure cannot be measured with the data I have. A set of macro forecasts priced in advance and scored in public as they resolve. A library of what I read and what I took from it.

Every number on this site traces to code that produced it. Where a claim rests on somebody else's figure, the paper says so and names them. Where a paper was corrected after publication, the correction is on the paper rather than quietly folded in, because a record that only ever improves is not a record.

What this is not

It is not a track record. No capital is at risk here and none of these results were earned in a live book, which is the one thing a backtest can never supply and I will not pretend otherwise. It is not investment advice, and nothing on this site is a recommendation to buy or sell anything. The universe of stocks it tests is survivorship-biased, which makes every raw performance figure an upper bound, and the papers say so in their own words rather than in a footnote.

Where I am going

I am looking for a quantitative research or investment risk seat where this way of working is the point rather than an eccentricity. If you have read a paper here and want to argue with it, that is the conversation I want, and the fastest way to have it is the link in the corner of this page.

The engine
Six stages, run nightly and unattended. Hypotheses, features, backtest, validation (Newey-West with a multiplicity-deflated bar, a block bootstrap, an out-of-sample decay limit, and a stressed rerun), regimes, and factor attribution. Every statistic is computed by deterministic code.
